# 1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The Kenya 30 Index (KEN30) is a blockchain-native benchmark designed to represent the performance of thirty leading companies listed on the Nairobi Securities Exchange (NSE).

KEN30 introduces a programmable, transparent, and tradable benchmark layer that enables real-time market exposure through digital asset infrastructure.

The objective is to position KEN30 as a market-facing index that can be integrated into centralized exchange trading environments, structured financial products, and institutional investment strategies.

KEN30 functions as a directional indicator of Kenya’s equity market and is architected to align with globally recognized benchmark standards.

# 12. REVENUE MODEL

KEN30 is designed to generate revenue through benchmark monetization, exchange infrastructure participation, structured product enablement, and institutional data distribution.

Revenue generation is not assumed to arise automatically from token trading itself. Instead, revenue is realized only where contractual, commercial, or structural arrangements are in place with exchanges, market makers, financial institutions, or data consumers.

The KEN30 revenue model is therefore based on **deliberate revenue capture architecture**, not passive token activity.

## 12.1 Exchange Revenue Sharing

The primary short-to-medium term commercial opportunity is exchange-linked revenue participation.

Where negotiated, KEN30 may participate in exchange economics through:

- trading fee sharing agreements
- volume-based rebate arrangements
- liquidity program incentives
- listing-linked commercial partnerships

Under this structure, revenue is derived from actual trading activity on exchanges where KEN30 is listed, subject to the terms of each exchange agreement.

This is the most direct mechanism through which exchange trading can generate issuer-level revenue.

## 12.2 Liquidity and Market Structure Participation

Additional revenue may be generated through participation in market structure operations.

Potential sources include:

- spread capture, where affiliated or designated liquidity operations are contractually structured to participate in market making economics
- exchange maker rebates
- liquidity incentive programs
- performance-based market structure compensation

This revenue stream is contingent on:

- active liquidity deployment
- sufficient trading volume
- compliant market making arrangements
- exchange-level incentive structures

This should be treated as a specialized, operational revenue stream rather than passive benchmark income.

## 12.3 Index Licensing

The most institutional and scalable long-term revenue stream for KEN30 is index licensing.

KEN30 may be licensed to:

- asset managers
- structured product issuers
- exchanges
- trading platforms
- research and analytics firms
- banks and broker-dealers

Licensing use cases may include:

- benchmark referencing

- synthetic market exposure products
- ETF-like or index-linked wrappers, subject to jurisdiction
- portfolio benchmarking
- market research and analytics

This is the benchmark business model most closely aligned with global index providers such as S&P Dow Jones Indices, MSCI, and FTSE Russell.

In long-term institutional terms, KEN30 should be viewed not merely as a tradable token, but as a benchmark business.

This positions KEN30 closer to an index infrastructure platform than to a conventional digital asset project.

Accordingly, the long-term economic objective is not only token trading activity, but the establishment of KEN30 as a monetizable benchmark layer within Kenya’s capital market infrastructure and, over time, within broader regional and global financial systems.

# 38. CORPORATE ACTIONS FRAMEWORK

Corporate actions affecting constituent securities require adjustments to maintain index integrity. These include stock splits, reverse splits, mergers, acquisitions, delistings, ticker changes, and restructurings. All such adjustments are executed through governance-approved updates to the on-chain constituent registry. Historical integrity is preserved through versioned registry updates.

---

# 39. TRADING SUSPENSION POLICY

Trading suspension conditions include:

- failure of oracle quorum validation  
- breach of maximum price staleness threshold  
- extreme market dislocation  
- governance-triggered emergency pause  

Upon suspension, trading may resume only after restoration of valid oracle updates or governance authorization.

---

# 40. ORACLE SERVICE LEVEL AGREEMENT (SLA)

Oracle operators are required to maintain:

- defined update intervals  
- minimum quorum participation  
- maximum staleness thresholds  
- deterministic price submission formats  

Failure to meet SLA conditions may trigger fallback or suspension mechanisms.

---

# 41. INDEX REVIEW AND REBALANCING POLICY

Index composition is subject to periodic review:

- quarterly review cycle  
- semi-annual rebalancing window  
- event-driven adjustments under governance  

All updates must pass through governance scheduling and timelock execution.

# 42. GLOSSARY

NAV: Net Asset Value representing weighted aggregate price  
Oracle: system responsible for submitting verified price updates  
Registry: on-chain storage of index constituents and weights  
Timelock: governance delay mechanism enforcing transparency  
Benchmark: reference index representing market performance
